# Explain

Explaining something hard in an easy way is the sign of true understanding. If you
cannot say it simply, you do not understand it well enough yet. Go back and find out
first.

## Mindset

- **Aim lower than you think.** Judge the reader's level, then explain a few levels
  below it. An expert loses nothing from a clear explanation. A beginner loses
  everything from a dense one.
- **Assume no context.** The reader has not seen your tool output, the files you read,
  or the terms you picked up on the way. Define each term the first time you use it, or
  replace it with plain words.
- **The point first.** Start with the answer in one or two sentences. Detail comes after,
  and only the detail that serves the point.
- **Concise is not cryptic.** Cut words, not steps. Every step the reader needs to follow
  the logic stays in. Every word that does not help them follow it goes.
- **Concrete beats abstract.** A real example, a small number, or an everyday analogy
  carries an idea further than a definition.

## Procedure

1. **Understand it yourself.** Read the code, the docs or the data until you can state
   the idea in one plain sentence. If you cannot, say what you are unsure of. Never cover
   a gap with jargon.
2. **Find the core.** Ask: if the reader keeps one thing, what is it? Write that
   sentence first.
3. **Build up from the ground.** Start from something the reader already knows. Add one
   new idea at a time. Each step uses only the steps before it.
4. **Make it concrete.** Give one example or analogy for the hardest step. Say where the
   analogy stops being true, if it can mislead.
5. **Show, when a picture is clearer.** A short list, a small table, or a simple diagram,
   when the shape of the idea is easier to see than to read. Not by default.
6. **Cut.** Re-read it as the reader. Remove every sentence they do not need, every
   hedge, every term you did not define. Keep it short enough to read in one go.
7. **Check the landing.** End with what the reader can now do or see, in one line. Offer
   to go deeper on a part, instead of going deeper unasked.

## Checks before you send

- Can someone with no context follow it from the first line?
- Is the answer in the first two sentences?
- Is every term either common or defined?
- Is there one concrete example for the hardest part?
- Is anything left that the reader does not need?

## Composition

- **The `technical-writing` rule**, at the conversational level, sets the register:
  short common words, active voice, no filler. This skill sets the level and the
  order.
- **Not for docs.** A README or a guide is the `docs` practice. Use the same values
  there, but follow that method.
